The Intel 4004 turns 55

This is the chip's actual circuit, running. Every one of its __N_TRANSISTORS__ transistors was recovered from tracings of the die's layers and is being switched on and off in your browser. The program it is running works out its own age and prints a birthday card.

From die to emulator

  1. Layers. The starting point is five tracings of the die: diffusion, polysilicon, metal, contacts and buried contacts, drawn by the 4004.com anniversary project from Intel's mask proofs and checked against photomicrographs like the one above.
  2. Transistors. Wherever polysilicon crosses diffusion there is a transistor, unless a buried contact joins them. Following every wire through every contact gives __N_TRANSISTORS__ transistors, __N_CAPS__ bootstrap capacitors and __N_NODES__ electrical nodes.
  3. Switches. Each transistor is treated as a switch. After every clock edge the simulator works out which wires are now connected to which supply, until nothing changes. No instruction set is programmed in anywhere.
  4. Checks. The chip was run against a conventional emulator written from the 1971 manual: every adder input, every accumulator instruction, RAM, jumps, and random programs. Every bus cycle matched.
  5. Photo. The layout was then lined up with the photograph by image correlation, to within __ALIGN_ERR__ pixels, so each wire's state can be painted where it really is.

How it works out its age

The 4004 was built for a desk calculator, so it has instructions for decimal arithmetic. The program subtracts 1971 from 2026 one decimal digit at a time, then lights a candle for each year: a row per ten, and a last row for the rest.

TCS      ; 9 or 10, depending on the borrow
SUB R4   ; minus a digit of 1971
CLC
ADD R8   ; plus a digit of 2026
DAA      ; back into decimal
XCH R8   ; keep the digit; carry = no borrow

It prints by writing half a character at a time to two output ports on the ROM chips. The 4004 has no halt instruction, so when the card is done it loops on a jump to itself.

What is and is not simulated

Simulated at transistor level: the 4004 itself. The pins you see lit beside the photo are read off the simulated bond pads.

Modelled by behaviour: the 4001 ROM and 4002 RAM chips around it, and the teletype.

Not attempted: analogue effects. Voltages are just on or off, and timing is an ordering of events, so this says nothing about how fast the real chip could be clocked. In a browser it manages a few thousand instruction cycles a second; the real one did 92,500.
