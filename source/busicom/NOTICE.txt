source/busicom/ -- third-party material for the Busicom 141-PF demo
=====================================================================

busicom-141pf.bin
  The Busicom 141-PF calculator firmware: 1280 bytes, addresses $000-$4FF (five 4001
  ROMs; the fifth, $400-$4FF, is the optional square-root ROM).
  SHA-1   6ab79dc4148e98888a5944580b12df9709a4826d
  SHA-256 c6af325d64b0895a8c6f7298e5443bbf5814783fabd6a1bcd2df08a9bec2ceb7

  Origin: binaries recovered by Tim McNerney and Fred Huettig in collaboration with the
  Computer History Museum (November 2005); disassembly and reverse engineering by Barry
  Silverman, Brian Silverman and Tim McNerney (November 2006); detailed analysis and
  comments by Lajos Kintli (July 2007). This image was rebuilt here from the address and
  byte columns of their annotated listing, release 1.0.1 (15 November 2009), with
  busicom/extract_rom.py (text parsing only).

  Licence: the listing's legal notice says: "The original Busicom binary code is not a
  copyrighted work and may be freely distributed without restriction." The image here
  is that binary code and nothing else.

The annotated listing itself is NOT kept in this tree. Download it from
  https://www.4004.com/2009/Busicom-141PF-Calculator_asm_rel-1-0-1.txt
  (linked from https://www.4004.com/busicom/ ; the copy used here had
  SHA-256 4b1b27167096ed466cf2a616c21dd1635d574253e4d9eb3c26d2516b9d791358)
and check that it gives the same image with
  make busicom-rom LISTING=path/to/Busicom-141PF-Calculator_asm_rel-1-0-1.txt

  Its comments and documentation are (c) the authors above, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 2.5
  (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/2.5/legalcode). That licence would
  allow a verbatim, attributed, non-commercial copy in this tree. It is left out anyway:
  nothing here needs it to build or test, so the tree redistributes only the
  uncopyrighted binary. The key names, printer character tables and the expected results
  of the listing's examples used in busicom/ and docs/BUSICOM.txt are short factual
  extracts, credited to the listing where they are used.

Also consulted, not included: Tim McNerney's Shinshu Seiki Model-102 printer emulator
(https://www.4004.com/assets/model-102-printer-sim.c, CC BY-NC-SA 2.5), for the printer
drum timing and signal phases.
